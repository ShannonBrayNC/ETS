# University-Neutral Doctoral Research Proposal

## Working title

**Independent Verification of Consequential Machine Actions: An Evidence Architecture for Distributed, AI and Cyber-Physical Systems**

## Research question

**Can a distributed system produce independently verifiable evidence sufficient for an independent verifier to reconstruct and evaluate the provenance, identity, authority, policy context, decision, action and resulting state of a consequential event without requiring trust in the originating system?**

## Background and motivation

Digital systems increasingly make or execute consequential decisions. This is especially visible in AI-assisted workflows, distributed services and autonomous or cyber-physical systems. Yet post-event accountability is frequently dependent on records generated by the same origin system whose behavior is under examination. Logs, signatures, provenance graphs, audit stores and attestation mechanisms can establish useful facts about integrity, identity, derivation or runtime state, but they do not automatically establish completeness, semantic truth, correct authority, actual execution or resulting consequence.

The central problem is therefore not simply how to create tamper-evident records. It is how to design an evidence architecture in which an independent verifier can determine which claims are cryptographically or procedurally supported, which remain asserted, which are contradicted and which are unknowable from the available evidence.

The proposed research investigates whether a common evidentiary model can preserve these distinctions across three increasingly difficult settings: conventional distributed software, AI-mediated machine action and cyber-physical action with externally observable consequences.

## Relationship to existing work

This research is deliberately positioned as complementary to, rather than a replacement for, established provenance, secure logging, attestation and assurance systems.

The W3C PROV family provides a general data model for representing provenance relationships among entities, activities and agents, with support for derivation, attribution, bundles and provenance-of-provenance. Secure audit-log research demonstrates techniques for making historical records resistant to undetected modification after compromise. Trusted execution and attestation mechanisms provide evidence about particular software/hardware measurements under defined trust assumptions. Assurance research for autonomous systems addresses the continuing problem of safety and functional confidence in learning-enabled cyber-physical systems. More recent work on AI and LLM audit trails extends lifecycle and governance traceability around models, data, deployments and decisions.

The proposed gap is narrower. Evidence Architecture asks whether evidence can be composed so that an independent verifier can explicitly separate:

- observation from inference;
- assertion from independently checkable evidence;
- integrity from semantic truth;
- identity from authority;
- authority from capability;
- command issuance from execution;
- intended result from observed resulting state;
- supported claims from unresolved trust assumptions;
- absence of evidence from evidence of absence.

The research does not claim novelty for provenance graphs, append-only logs, hashes, signatures, Merkle structures, chain of custody, event histories or attestation individually. Novelty must instead be established, revised or rejected through a systematic closest-work analysis of the proposed compositional model and its verification semantics.

## Research objectives

The project has six primary objectives.

1. **Define a bounded evidence model.** Determine the minimum representation required for an Evidence Object and related graph structure to support independent evaluation of identity, integrity, provenance, custody, authority, policy context and verification state without implying semantic truth.

2. **Formalize trust decomposition.** Specify which conclusions follow from cryptographic or procedural evidence and which remain dependent on sensors, identity providers, hardware roots, institutions, external observers or other assumptions.

3. **Evaluate distributed evidence continuity.** Determine whether evidentiary properties remain meaningful under partition, asynchronous transport, duplication, delay, reordering and later synchronization.

4. **Evaluate AI machine-action provenance.** Determine what externally inspectable evidence is sufficient to reconstruct consequential AI-assisted action without requiring access to hidden internal reasoning traces.

5. **Evaluate cyber-physical provenance and consequence custody.** Determine whether a verifier can distinguish observation, inference, decision, authority, command, actuator execution and independently observed resulting physical state.

6. **Establish failure boundaries.** Determine under which attacker capabilities or system faults Evidence Architecture properties remain sound, degrade detectably or fail.

## Research questions and hypotheses

The canonical programme contains RQ0 through RQ8. The strongest initial hypothesis families are:

- canonicalized and cryptographically bound Evidence Objects can preserve deterministic identity and integrity across conforming implementations;
- completeness cannot generally be inferred from an origin-produced evidence object without an external expectation model or independent observation channel;
- typed evidentiary relationships can preserve provenance structure while keeping each relationship attributable and independently challengeable;
- explicit trust decomposition can prevent integrity from being misrepresented as truth;
- bounded offline capture and deterministic reconciliation can preserve meaningful provenance under explicit fairness/healing assumptions;
- machine-action evidence can preserve externally inspectable inputs, model/runtime identity, policy, decision output, authority, action and consequence without hidden chain-of-thought;
- authenticated command evidence is insufficient to prove actuator execution or physical consequence;
- independent observation at multiple boundaries can strengthen attribution and expose some classes of omission or contradiction;
- some origin-side omission attacks are fundamentally undetectable without independent expectations or witnesses.

Each hypothesis may be supported, narrowed, revised or refuted.

## Methodology

The doctorate will combine systematic literature analysis, formal methods, experimental systems research and adversarial evaluation.

### 1. Literature and novelty qualification

A structured review will cover provenance models, W3C PROV, secure/tamper-evident logging, digital forensics and chain of custody, event sourcing, software supply-chain provenance, remote attestation and TEEs, dependable systems, assurance cases, responsible-AI logging, autonomous-system assurance and cyber-physical provenance.

For each candidate contribution, the closest prior work will be identified and a claim-difference table maintained. Candidate contributions that collapse into established prior art will be narrowed or retired.

### 2. Formalization

Evidence Object, Evidence Graph, verification-state, trust-boundary and consequence-custody semantics will be expressed formally enough to state invariants and nonclaims. Existing TLA+/Alloy work will be refined where suitable. Formal results will be clearly separated from empirical conclusions.

### 3. Distributed-system experiments

Prospective experiments will exercise canonicalization, independent verification, partition, delay, duplication, reordering, replay, omission, manipulation and later reconciliation. Multiple verifier implementations should be used where practical to test representation independence and reconstruction agreement.

### 4. AI machine-action experiments

AI-mediated workloads will record model/runtime identity, externally visible inputs/context, policy, authorization, declared output, tool call or action, and externally observed result. Experiments will vary model versions, nondeterministic outputs, omitted events, stale context, policy changes and tool failures.

The research explicitly avoids treating hidden chain-of-thought as required evidence.

### 5. Cyber-physical experiments

Ranger R0, or an equivalent instrumented research platform, will provide prospective experiments across the chain:

`Observation -> Inference -> Decision -> Authority -> Action -> Resulting State`

Controlled failures will include sensor disagreement, delayed/stale telemetry, command rejection, actuator non-execution, contradictory observers, authority changes and transport faults. Command evidence will be evaluated separately from execution and resulting-state evidence.

### 6. Adversarial qualification and replication

A defined attacker/fault model will test where evidentiary properties survive, degrade detectably or fail. Negative results and counterexamples will be retained as first-class outputs. Reproducible experiment packages will be released and independent reproduction sought where practical.

## Data and evidence

The project does not initially depend on access to a proprietary institutional dataset. Core research data can be generated prospectively from controlled systems and includes:

- Evidence Objects and typed graph relationships;
- cryptographic/verification metadata;
- system and independent-observer telemetry;
- policy and authority state;
- AI runtime/model identity and action records;
- sensor, command, actuator and resulting-state observations;
- injected-fault ground truth;
- verifier decisions and reconstruction outputs;
- performance, latency and storage measurements.

External datasets or institutional collaborations may later improve ecological validity but are not required to establish the core research design.

## Evaluation

Evaluation will include both correctness-oriented and operational measures:

- reconstruction completeness under a declared evidence expectation model;
- correct classification of verified, asserted, contradicted and unknown claims;
- detection rate for manipulations that violate declared invariants;
- false-confidence/false-attribution rate;
- continuity across asynchronous transport and reconciliation;
- agreement among independent verifier implementations;
- ability to distinguish command from execution and consequence;
- behavior as independent observation channels are removed or compromised;
- computational, storage and latency overhead;
- third-party reproducibility.

A valid outcome is that particular claims cannot be established from available evidence. The system should report that limitation rather than silently promote an assertion into a fact.

## Expected contribution

The expected contribution is a rigorously bounded model and experimental body of evidence for independently verifiable machine-action provenance across software, AI and cyber-physical systems.

The strongest potential contribution is not a single cryptographic primitive. It is the synthesis of evidence representation, independently attributable relationships, verification state, uncertainty, trust decomposition, authority/policy context and consequence custody into a common architecture whose claims remain explicitly bounded by their evidence.

The research will be considered successful even if some proposed ETS contributions are narrowed or refuted, provided the work establishes defensible boundaries describing what can and cannot be independently verified.

## Prospective publication plan

A prospective-publication route could naturally produce a coherent sequence of papers:

1. Evidence Architecture foundations and trust decomposition.
2. Evidence Objects and Evidence Graph semantics.
3. Independent verification under distributed/offline conditions.
4. Evidence for AI-mediated machine action.
5. Cyber-physical provenance and consequence custody.
6. Adversarial qualification, failure boundaries and reproducibility.

The final thesis synthesis would explain the overarching question, literature context, cross-paper method, surviving contributions, negative results and limitations.

## Feasibility and preliminary work

A substantial preliminary research platform already exists in the ETS repository, including canonical research questions, a contribution ledger, experiment ledger, formal architecture/evidence theory, first-pass prior-art analysis, distributed/offline evidence implementation, AI Witness architecture, Black Box architecture, formal-model artifacts, reproducibility infrastructure and the Ranger cyber-physical research programme.

These artifacts establish feasibility and research maturity. They are not treated as proof that the doctoral hypotheses are true. The decisive literature qualification, prospective experiment protocols, datasets, peer-reviewed publications, independent reproduction and thesis synthesis remain research work to be completed under doctoral supervision.
