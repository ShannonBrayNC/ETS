## Study title

Comparative evaluation of evidence-representation formats for reconstructing events and decisions.

## Why this study is being done

This research examines whether different ways of presenting the same underlying evidence help people reconstruct what happened, what was authorized, what remains uncertain, and what consequences were actually observed.

The study compares several evidence-representation formats while holding the substantive scenario facts constant.

## What participation would involve

If you choose to participate, you would review 12 short technical or operational scenarios. For each scenario you would receive one evidence-representation format and answer six fixed questions about what the evidence supports and what remains uncertain.

You would also be asked to provide a confidence rating from 1 to 5. The time taken to respond may be recorded.

You would not be asked to search the internet or other external sources. It is acceptable to answer that a conclusion is unknown, unavailable, contradictory, or indeterminate when the evidence does not support a stronger conclusion.

## Risks or discomforts

Possible risks include ordinary reading fatigue, frustration with ambiguous scenarios, or mild performance-related discomfort. There is also a privacy risk if responses are linked to personal identity.

The study does not involve a physical intervention or operational control of real systems.

## Privacy

The research design intends to use a study code rather than public participant identity in analytic datasets. Contact or recruitment information should be stored separately from response data where practical and as required by the governing institution.

Individual performance should not be publicly reported by name.

The final data-retention, access, and deletion/archive rules will be provided before participation.

## Benefits

There may be no direct personal benefit to you. The study may contribute to research on how evidence should be represented for more accurate reconstruction of administrative, automated, and cyber-physical events.
