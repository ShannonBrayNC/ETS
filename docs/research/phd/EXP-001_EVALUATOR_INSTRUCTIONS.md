## Purpose

You will review a series of evidence packages describing events or decisions. Your task is to reconstruct what the available evidence supports without assuming facts that are not established.

This is not a test of your knowledge of any named evidence framework. The representations may differ in structure and terminology. Treat each package on its own terms.

## General rules

1. Use only the evidence shown in the package.
2. Distinguish direct observations from inferences or conclusions.
3. Do not assume that a valid signature proves factual truth, legal authority, completeness, or physical outcome unless the package supplies evidence for that specific claim.
4. Do not assume that a command or acknowledgment proves execution or resulting consequence.
5. Do not assume that missing evidence proves an underlying event did not occur unless the package establishes that the evidence source had complete observation coverage.
6. If evidence is contradictory, stale, unavailable, or insufficient, say so explicitly.
7. It is acceptable to answer “unknown,” “not established,” or “indeterminate.”
8. Do not search external sources or use information not present in the package.

## Fixed questions

Answer all six questions for every scenario:

1. What events or states are directly supported by the evidence?
2. What conclusions are inferred rather than directly observed?
3. Which actor/action possessed valid standing at the relevant time, if established?
4. What action was requested, what action was executed, and what consequence/result was actually observed?
5. What material facts remain unknown, unavailable, contradictory, stale, or unverified?
6. What is the strongest defensible overall conclusion without exceeding the evidence?

## Response form

Use concise prose. Cite the evidence item or relationship that supports each important conclusion when possible.

If you cannot distinguish two events because timing, authority, completeness, or observation quality is insufficient, state that limitation rather than choosing one.

## Confidence

After question 6, provide a confidence rating from 1 to 5:

- 1 — very uncertain
- 2 — uncertain
- 3 — moderate confidence
- 4 — confident
- 5 — very confident

Confidence should reflect the strength and completeness of the evidence, not familiarity with the domain.

## Neutrality notice

The packages may use different schemas, relation names, or visual structures. No representation should be assumed to be more trustworthy because it appears more detailed, formal, technical, or cryptographic.

Your task is to determine the strongest claims supported by the evidence actually presented.
