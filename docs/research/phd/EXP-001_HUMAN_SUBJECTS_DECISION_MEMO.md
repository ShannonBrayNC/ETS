# EXP-001 Human-Subjects Decision Memo

**Status:** pre-recruitment decision support; not an institutional determination  
**Experiment:** EXP-001  
**Execution state:** NOT EXECUTED

## Bottom line

EXP-001 is designed as a systematic investigation intended to contribute to generalizable knowledge. If human evaluators are recruited and their responses, performance, timing, confidence, background, or other information are collected and analyzed, the activity is likely to meet the Common Rule concept of research involving interaction with living individuals.

This repository document does **not** determine exemption, non-human-subject status, or IRB approval. That determination must be made under the requirements of the institution or sponsor governing the doctoral research.

## Regulatory frame

The HHS Common Rule defines research as a systematic investigation designed to develop or contribute to generalizable knowledge. It defines a human subject as a living individual about whom an investigator conducting research obtains information through intervention or interaction and uses/studies/analyzes that information, or obtains identifiable private information/biospecimens.

EXP-001 prospectively collects evaluator responses to research tasks specifically to compare representation conditions. That design should therefore be treated conservatively as potentially human-subjects research until an appropriate institutional authority determines otherwise.

## Why de-identification alone does not settle the question

The study can minimize privacy risk by using evaluator codes and avoiding unnecessary identifiers, but interaction with participants can itself satisfy the human-subject definition. Anonymous or minimally identifiable data may affect risk and exemption analysis; it does not by itself prove that the activity is outside human-subjects research.

## Potential low-risk/exempt pathway

Depending on the governing institution and exact implementation, a minimal-risk adult evaluator study using benign analytical tasks, surveys/tests, and non-sensitive response data may potentially fit an exemption category or expedited/minimal-risk pathway.

The investigator SHALL NOT self-certify an exemption when the institution requires IRB or designated-office determination.

## Required pre-recruitment actions

Before inviting evaluators for confirmatory data collection:

1. identify the university/institution under which EXP-001 would be performed or submitted;
2. obtain its written human-research/IRB policy and determination procedure;
3. submit the protocol for the required determination (not-human-subjects, exempt, expedited, or full review as applicable);
4. preserve the determination letter/record in the research archive;
5. freeze any required consent/information sheet before recruitment;
6. define what participant metadata will be collected and why;
7. minimize identifiers and sensitive data;
8. define retention, access, withdrawal, and publication handling;
9. do not describe the study as IRB-approved or exempt unless the responsible authority has made that determination.

## Proposed privacy-minimizing implementation

Unless an institutional protocol requires otherwise, collect only:

- evaluator code;
- broad experience stratum;
- scenario responses;
- elapsed reconstruction time;
- optional confidence score;
- condition/assignment metadata.

Do not collect date of birth, home address, medical information, political views, SSN, financial information, employer-specific confidential data, or unrelated sensitive attributes.

If email/contact information is needed for scheduling, store it separately from response data and do not include it in analytical exports.

## Consent/information-sheet minimum topics

If required by the institutional determination, participant-facing information should disclose:

- purpose in neutral terms;
- expected task and duration;
- voluntary participation;
- ability to stop where applicable;
- categories of data collected;
- foreseeable risks (expected to be low for the proposed task);
- confidentiality/data-handling approach;
- compensation, if any;
- contact mechanism for questions;
- institutional/IRB contact language when required.

The information sheet should not reveal which format is expected to perform better.

## Special populations

The confirmatory evaluator pool SHOULD be adults capable of consent. Do not recruit children, prisoners, or other populations carrying additional regulatory protections unless an approved protocol specifically includes them.

## Internal dry runs

Mechanical dry runs by the author or engineering collaborators may test rendering, broken links, timing instrumentation, or data-capture plumbing. They SHALL NOT be represented as confirmatory human-subject results and SHALL NOT be pooled with later confirmatory data unless prospectively permitted by the governing protocol.

All non-human pre-execution work—packet rendering, hashing, equivalence review, assignment generation, scoring implementation, and analysis-code preparation—may continue without collecting evaluator outcome data.
